What makes Yishun, Yishun

Yishun is one of the north's original new towns, with development starting in 1981. It's big, established and still growing, mixing older blocks with brand-new neighbourhoods.

Everything at Northpoint City

Mall, bus interchange, library and community club in one air-conditioned complex next to Yishun MRT. A lot of daily life happens without leaving it.

A straight line to town

Yishun and Khatib are both on the North-South Line, so you get a direct ride to Orchard and the city without changing trains.

Healthcare on your doorstep

Khoo Teck Puat Hospital and Yishun Community Hospital sit in the town, and more healthcare facilities are planned nearby.

Water and green

Lower Seletar Reservoir, Yishun Park and Yishun Pond Park give you proper space for walks, cycling and weekend fishing.

Old and new side by side

Established 1980s blocks near the town centre, and new estates like Chencharu near Khatib. There's a wide spread of prices and flat ages.

Still evolving

The Draft Master Plan 2025 adds new homes around Yishun MRT, including the former temporary bus interchange site.

Who Yishun suits

  • Families who want more space for their budget than towns nearer the city

  • Couples who want to stay near parents already living in the north

Selling in Yishun: what buyers look at

  • Distance to Yishun or Khatib MRT and to Northpoint City is the first question buyers ask.
  • With many 1980s blocks, remaining lease matters: it affects how much CPF and loan buyers can use, so we price with that in mind.
  • Older flats sell better when buyers can picture a renovation: clear photos, a sensible price and renovation ideas help.
  • Newer flats near Khatib compete with BTO supply, so condition and floor level carry more weight.

How to choose the best property agent in Yishun

"Best" means different things to different families. Whoever you speak to, me included, here's what I'd check:

  • Ask what they've sold nearby, and for how much. Recent, real transactions near your block tell you more than any slogan. Ask how they priced them and how long they took.
  • Check their reviews and CEA registration. Look for recent Google reviews from real clients, and check the agent's name on the CEA Public Register.
  • Ask them to explain your numbers. Not just the selling price: your CPF refund, cash proceeds and timeline. If they can't explain it simply, keep looking.
  • Be careful with the highest valuation. An exciting number can be a way to win your listing. Ask what it's based on.
  • Ask how they'll market your home. Photos, video, portals, social media, and who will actually be at the viewings.
  • Pick someone you can talk to. You'll be messaging this person for months. Make sure they reply, explain, and don't pressure you.
