Important: please read
  • This calculator gives estimates for planning only. It is not a loan offer, an approval, or financial advice. Banks and HDB decide your actual loan amount.
  • It applies the rules as at 24 November 2026: TDSR 55% of gross income; MSR 30% for HDB flats and ECs; a stress-test rate of at least 4% for bank loans and 3% for HDB loans; LTV 75% (55% if tenure exceeds 25 years for HDB flats or 30 years for private property, or the loan runs past age 65); and minimum cash of 5% (10% at 55% LTV) for bank loans.
  • It assumes no other outstanding housing loans. A second housing loan has much lower LTV limits. ABSD, renovation, and property tax aren't included.
  • Banks typically count only part of variable or self-employed income. HDB loan eligibility also depends on household income ceilings, citizenship and other conditions not modelled here.
  • Before committing, get an HFE letter (for HDB) or an in-principle approval from a bank, and check the latest rules with MAS, HDB, CPF and IRAS.

About these numbers

Why is my maximum loan lower than I expected?

Banks don't test your loan at today's interest rate. MAS requires them to use at least 4% (HDB uses 3% for its own loans) to check you could still afford it if rates rise. That stress test is what shrinks the number.

What's the difference between TDSR and MSR?

TDSR caps all your monthly debt repayments (home, car, other loans) at 55% of income. MSR caps the home loan alone at 30% of income, and applies only to HDB flats and ECs bought from developers.

Why did my LTV drop to 55%?

For bank loans, the 75% LTV applies only if the tenure is within 25 years (HDB flats) or 30 years (private), and the loan ends by age 65. Otherwise it's 55%, and you need at least 10% in cash.

I'm self-employed or earn commission. Does this work for me?

Banks usually count only part of variable income such as commission, bonuses and self-employed income. Your actual figure may be lower. Talk to a mortgage banker for an in-principle approval.
