Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD): rates, the 4-year rule, and a quick check

What SSD is, when it applies, and how to know your SSD-free date before you buy.

By Andik Imran, CEA R061801FLast reviewed 24 November 2026
Short answerSeller's Stamp Duty (SSD) is a tax on selling a residential property soon after buying it. For properties bought on or after 4 July 2025, it applies if you sell within 4 years: 16% in the first year, 12% in the second, 8% in the third and 4% in the fourth. It doesn't affect HDB flats, because their MOP is longer.

SSD matters most to upgraders who buy a condo or new launch and might need to sell within a few years: a job move, a family change, or a better opportunity. Know the date you're "free" before you buy.

Who should think about SSD

  • New launch buyers: the clock starts when you buy, not at TOP.
  • Resale condo buyers who might relocate, upgrade again or downsize within 4 years.
  • Investors planning a short holding period.
Before you buy private: I write down your SSD-free date alongside your SAFE plan, so a future decision is never made without it.

Questions families ask me

Does SSD apply when I sell my HDB flat?

In practice, no. The revised SSD doesn't affect HDB owners, because the HDB Minimum Occupation Period is longer than the SSD holding period.

Is SSD calculated on my selling price?

On the selling price or the market value, whichever is higher.

When does the holding period start and end?

Generally from the date you bought (when you exercised the Option or signed the S&P) to the date you sell (when you grant the Option or sign the sale agreement). Your lawyer confirms the exact dates.

Who pays SSD, buyer or seller?

The seller.
