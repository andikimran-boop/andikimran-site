Selling an inherited HDB flat: faraid, wills and what to do first

When a parent passes on, the flat raises hard questions. Here's the order to take them in, and how to keep the family together while you do.

By Andik Imran, CEA R061801FLast reviewed 24 November 2026
Short answerFirst, find out how the flat was held (joint tenancy or tenancy-in-common) and whether there's a will. For a Muslim owner, you'll usually need an Inheritance Certificate from the Syariah Court. For non-Muslims, it's a Grant of Probate (with a will) or Letters of Administration (without). Only then can the flat be transferred or sold, and HDB will check whether the people inheriting are eligible to own it.

Losing a parent is hard enough. Then comes the flat: who owns it now, who's living in it, and what the family should do. I've sat with many families at this point. The paperwork is rarely the hardest part. Getting everyone on the same page is.

Step one: how was the flat held?

Check the ownership type. It changes everything.

  • Joint tenancy (common for married couples): when one owner passes away, their share goes to the surviving owner. This applies to Muslim joint tenants too, so faraid doesn't apply to the flat in this case.
  • Tenancy-in-common, or a sole owner: the deceased's share becomes part of their estate and is distributed according to their will or, without one, the law of inheritance.

Not sure which applies? HDB can confirm it, and so can your lawyer.

If the owner was Muslim: faraid

A Muslim estate is distributed under faraid. If there's no will, the whole share follows faraid. If there is a will, the owner can only give away up to one-third through it, and the remaining two-thirds still follows faraid.

The family applies to the Syariah Court for an Inheritance Certificate, which sets out each beneficiary's share. The Syariah Court's website also has an online trial calculator that gives you a sense of the shares before you apply.

If the owner was not Muslim

With a will, the executor applies for a Grant of Probate. Without a will, the Intestate Succession Act decides who inherits, and a family member applies for Letters of Administration to manage the estate.

Can the family keep the flat?

Sometimes. After the beneficiaries are identified, HDB checks whether they're eligible to own the flat. A common snag is a beneficiary who already owns another flat. If no one eligible wants to keep it, the flat is usually sold and the proceeds shared out.

If there's still a bank loan on the flat, the bank will also check whether the person taking over can afford it under its lending rules.

Selling an inherited flat: what actually happens

  1. Get the death certificate and gather the flat documents.
  2. Apply for the Inheritance Certificate, Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration.
  3. Sit down as a family: agree the price range, timeline, and plans for anyone living there.
  4. Get a realistic valuation from recent sales in the block and estate.
  5. Prepare the flat, list it, hold viewings, negotiate.
  6. The executor or administrator signs the Option to Purchase and the HDB resale application.
  7. On completion, the proceeds are distributed according to the Inheritance Certificate, will or grant.

The part nobody warns you about

Most delays aren't legal. They're emotional. One sibling wants to keep Mak's flat, another needs the money now, someone has been living there for years. When it goes wrong, families end up in court.

What I do differently: I act as the one neutral person in the middle. Every sibling sees the same price evidence, the same offers and the same updates, in writing, so no one feels left out or worries that someone else knows more.

Questions families ask me

Can we sell the flat before the Inheritance Certificate or grant is out?

The person with legal authority (the administrator or executor) needs to be in place to sign the sale. While you wait, we can prepare everything else: valuation, family agreement on price, and the timeline for whoever is living there.

Do all the beneficiaries need to agree to sell?

The administrator or executor signs, but in my experience the sale goes far more smoothly when every beneficiary agrees on the price range and timeline first. Disagreements are what drag these sales out.

My sibling is still living in the flat. What now?

This is common and needs care. Agree early, as a family, on when they'll move and where to. If it can't be settled amicably, speak to a lawyer, since these disputes can end up in court.

How long does it take?

Mostly it depends on how long the Inheritance Certificate or grant takes and how quickly the family agrees. Once that's in place, the sale itself runs like any other HDB resale.

Who do I talk to first: a lawyer or an agent?

For the will, grant or Inheritance Certificate, a lawyer or the Syariah Court. For what the flat is worth and what selling involves, me. I'm happy to work alongside your lawyer.
