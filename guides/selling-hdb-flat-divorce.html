Selling your HDB flat during a divorce

How the decision to sell or keep is made, what MOP means for you, where the money goes, and how to sell without another fight.

By Andik Imran, CEA R061801FLast reviewed 24 November 2026
Short answerWhether the flat is sold or kept, and how the money is split, is set out in the ancillary matters order, either agreed between you or decided by the court. To sell on the open market, the flat generally needs to have met its MOP by the date the divorce is final; otherwise you'll need HDB's approval. Keeping the flat depends on meeting HDB's eligibility rules, separately from what the court decides.

If you're reading this, things are probably heavy right now. Let's keep the flat part simple. Here's how it usually works, and how to sell without it becoming another battle.

Sell or keep: where it's decided

The flat is usually part of the matrimonial assets. Unless you reach a written agreement, the court decides at the ancillary matters stage whether the flat is sold or kept, who keeps it, and how the proceeds are divided. It looks at things like each person's contributions, the children's needs and the length of the marriage.

If one of you wants to keep it

Even if the court agrees, HDB has its own eligibility rules for keeping the flat. These look at citizenship, the children's care and control, and the scheme you'd keep it under. If neither of you is eligible, the flat will usually have to be sold.

Special case: if the marriage is annulled, or an engaged couple breaks up before marrying, neither person can usually keep the flat.

MOP: the date that matters

To sell on the open market, the flat should have met its Minimum Occupation Period by the date the divorce is final. If it hasn't, you'll need to ask HDB for approval, and your options may be narrower. Get this checked early.

Where the money goes

When the flat is sold, the outstanding loan is paid off first and the CPF used for the flat is refunded to each person's CPF account, with accrued interest. The remaining cash is split according to the court order.

HDB will ask for the divorce documents when you sell: the Interim or Final Judgment and the relevant Order of Court, or the Syariah Court decree or certificate for Muslim divorces.

How to keep the sale calm

  • One agent, two clients. I keep you both informed equally, in writing.
  • Agree the rules, not just the price. A listing range, a floor price, and how quickly you'll respond to offers.
  • Plan viewings around real life. Especially if one of you has moved out, or the kids are at home.
  • Watch the court's deadlines. Orders often set a timeline to sell. We plan backwards from it.
A gentle note: I'm not a lawyer, and you'll need one for the divorce itself. I take care of the property side and work with your lawyers so you don't have to relay messages between everyone.

Questions families ask me

Do we both need to sign to sell?

Yes, both owners normally sign the sale documents. If one party refuses after the court has ordered the sale, the court order can allow someone else, such as a court officer, to sign on their behalf. Your lawyer will advise.

Can I keep the flat after the divorce?

Possibly. The court decides who gets what, but HDB separately decides whether you're eligible to keep the flat, for example based on your citizenship, the children and the scheme you'd keep it under. Check with HDB before you agree on terms.

We haven't reached MOP. Are we stuck?

Not necessarily. If MOP isn't met by the time the divorce is final, you'll generally need HDB's approval to sell on the open market. Talk to HDB and your lawyer early, as this affects your options.

How do we agree on the selling price when we don't agree on anything?

Agree on the method, not the number. For example: list within a range based on recent sales, and accept any offer above an agreed floor. I share every offer with both of you in writing.

Will you take sides?

No. I'm there to sell the flat well and keep both of you informed. If communication needs to go through your lawyers, I'll work that way.
