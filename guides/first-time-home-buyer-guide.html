First-time home buyer guide: from HFE letter to keys

Everything a first-timer couple needs, in the order you'll need it: eligibility, grants, budget, the resale steps and what you pay at each one.

By Andik Imran, CEA R061801FLast reviewed 24 November 2026
Short answerStart with the HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter. It tells you whether you can buy, which grants you get, and how much HDB will lend you. With it in hand, you know your real budget before you fall in love with a flat. Eligible first-timer families buying a resale flat can receive up to $230,000 in CPF housing grants.

Buying your first home is exciting and a little terrifying. The good news: the process is well-defined, and as a first-timer you get the most help the government gives. Here's how it works, in order.

Grant amounts depend on your citizenship, income and flat type. Your HFE letter states exactly what you qualify for.

The steps, in order

  1. Week 0

    Check your eligibility and apply for the HFE letter

    Done on the HDB Flat Portal. It can take up to a month once your documents are in, so start early.

  2. While you wait

    Set your budget

    Your HDB loan or bank in-principle approval, your CPF OA, your cash and the grants. Try my loan calculator. Leave a buffer for renovation.

  3. Weeks 4–8

    View flats and agree a price

    Check the remaining lease, the block's recent transactions, and what's nearby: schools, parents, MRT.

  4. Option date

    BuyingThe seller grants you an Option to Purchase

    You need a valid HFE letter at this point. You then have 21 calendar days to decide.

    Option fee up to $1,000 · cash

  5. Within 21 days

    BuyingExercise the Option

    You commit to the purchase.

    Option exercise fee · cash (deposit up to $5,000 in total)

  6. Within 14 days of exercising

    BuyingPay Buyer's Stamp Duty

    Usually handled through your lawyer.

    Buyer's Stamp Duty

  7. After exercising

    Submit the resale application with the seller

    HDB reviews it and issues its acceptance.

  8. About 8 weeks after HDB's acceptance

    BuyingCompletion and keys

    Your downpayment, legal fees and loan are settled, and the grants are credited towards the purchase. Your monthly instalment starts.

    Downpayment balance · CPF/cashLegal fees

Costs first-timers forget

  • Renovation and furniture, usually the biggest surprise
  • Legal and conveyancing fees
  • Home and fire insurance
  • A buffer of a few months' expenses that you don't touch
My advice to first-timers: buy for the next 5–10 years, not just the next 2. If kids are coming, think about schools and grandparents nearby. The Proximity Housing Grant can make "near Mak" the cheaper choice too.

Questions families ask me

Should we go BTO or resale?

BTO is cheaper but you wait several years and pick from where HDB is building. Resale costs more but you can move in within months, choose the exact estate (near parents, schools, work), and stack up to three grants. Many couples who need space now, or want to be near family, find resale makes more sense.

How long does it take to buy a resale flat?

Plan for about three to four months: up to a month for the HFE letter, a few weeks to find the flat, 21 days on the Option, then about 8 weeks from HDB's acceptance of the resale application to completion.

Do we need cash, or can we use CPF?

You'll need some cash: the option fee and deposit (up to $5,000 in total), plus at least 5% of the price in cash if you take a bank loan. With an HDB loan, the downpayment can be paid fully from CPF. Stamp duty and legal fees can usually be paid or reimbursed from CPF. Your lawyer confirms the details.

Do we have to pay back the grants if we sell?

When you eventually sell, the grants (with the interest they would have earned) go back into your CPF accounts, not to the government. It's still your money, for your next home or retirement.

How much can we borrow?

Use my loan calculator for a quick estimate. Your HFE letter will confirm your HDB loan eligibility.
